# Prevention of Sexual Harassment (PoSH)

**Sources:** MountBlue PoSH Policy Document, Sexual Harassment Overview, Bullying & Harassment, and Workplace Scenarios Training Modules.

---

## 1. What Constitutes Sexual Harassment?

Sexual harassment is any **unwelcome** verbal, visual, digital, or physical conduct of a sexual nature that violates a person's dignity or creates an intimidating, hostile, or offensive work environment.

> **Core Principle:** **Intent does not matter; impact does.** Even if a comment or action was meant as a joke, it constitutes harassment if it is unwelcome and offensive to the recipient or a bystander.

### Forms of Unwelcome Behaviour

* **Verbal**
  * Making comments about someone’s body, appearance, or clothing.
  * Telling sexual or gender-based jokes, making innuendos, or using obscene language.
  * Repeatedly asking someone out for dates or social meetups after they have declined.
  * Asking intrusive questions about someone’s personal or sexual life, or spreading rumours about it.
* **Visual & Digital (Including Remote/Hybrid Work)**
  * Displaying sexually suggestive posters, cartoons, memes, or screensavers.
  * Sending unwelcome emails, text messages, GIFs, or voicemails of a sexual nature.
  * Cyberbullying, inappropriate comments during video calls, or unwanted late-night messaging across work or social platforms.
* **Physical**
  * Unwelcome touching, hugging, patting, stroking, or intentionally brushing against someone.
  * Blocking someone’s path or invading their personal space.
  * Leering, staring, whistling, or making sexually suggestive gestures.
  * Any form of physical molestation or sexual assault.

### Two Legal Categories of Harassment

1. **Quid Pro Quo ("This for That"):** When a person in a position of authority explicitly or implicitly demands sexual favours in exchange for workplace benefits (such as hiring, promotions, raises, or favourable projects) or threatens negative consequences (such as demotion, poor appraisals, or termination) if refused.
2. **Hostile Work Environment:** Repeated or severe unwelcome conduct that interferes with an individual’s work performance or creates an intimidating, humiliating, or offensive atmosphere. This is the most common form of workplace harassment.

### Related Misconduct to Avoid

While distinct from sexual harassment, MountBlue strictly prohibits related behaviours that undermine a safe workplace:

* **Gender-Based Discrimination:** Stereotyping, assigning inferior tasks or unequal workloads based on gender, blocking career advancement, or dismissing concerns based on gender bias.
* **Workplace Bullying:** Yelling, name-calling, public humiliation, intimidation, or harmful hazing.

### Important Clarifications

* **Anyone can be involved:** Harassment can occur regardless of gender (same-gender or opposite-gender) and can involve supervisors, peers, subordinates, vendors, clients, or visitors.
* **Extended Workplace:** The policy applies not only in the physical office, but also during off-sites, client visits, work-related travel, company-sponsored social events, and online/remote interactions.
* **Bystander Impact:** Conduct can still qualify as harassment even if the direct target did not immediately object, or if a bystander is offended by the behaviour.
* **What is *not* harassment:** Routine, respectful management actions—such as assigning duties, setting deadlines, giving constructive performance feedback, or enforcing attendance—do not constitute harassment.

## 2. What Should I Do If I Face or Witness Harassment?

### If You Are Experiencing Harassment

1. **Set a clear boundary (if safe to do so):** Tell the person firmly and respectfully that their behaviour is unwelcome and ask them to stop immediately.
2. **Document everything:** Keep a written log of dates, times, locations, what was said or done, any witnesses present, and how you responded. Save relevant emails, screenshots, or messages.
3. **File a written complaint with the Internal Complaints Committee (ICC):** If the behaviour continues, or if you do not feel comfortable confronting the person directly, submit a written complaint (ideally within **3 months** of the incident) to any member of MountBlue’s ICC:
   * **Bipasha Agarwal (Presiding Officer):** bipasha@mountblue.io
   * **N Gangadhar Reddy:** gangadhar@mountblue.io
   * **Konark:** konark@mountblue.io
4. **Seek external legal recourse if needed:** If the incident involves a criminal offence (such as assault or stalking), you also have the right to file a police complaint, and the ICC will assist you in doing so if requested.

### If You Witness Harassment

* **Speak up—reporting is a shared responsibility:** Being a silent bystander enables a toxic culture. If you witness harassment or are informed about an incident, support the affected colleague, note down what you observed, and report it to the ICC.
* **Third-party complaints:** With the aggrieved person’s consent (or in specific circumstances where they are unable to file it themselves), a co-worker or witness can help bring the matter to the ICC's attention.

## 3. What to Expect After Reporting

* **Zero-Tolerance Policy:** MountBlue treats every complaint seriously and conducts a fair, unbiased inquiry in line with the PoSH Act.
* **Strict Confidentiality:** All complaints, identities, and inquiry proceedings are kept strictly confidential. Breaching confidentiality is itself a punishable offence.
* **Protection Against Retaliation:** Retaliating against, intimidating, or penalizing anyone who files a complaint or participates in an inquiry as a witness is strictly prohibited.
* **Disciplinary Consequences:** If an allegation is substantiated, the offender may face actions ranging from:
  * A formal warning or mandatory written apology
  * Mandatory counselling or sensitivity training
  * Withholding of promotions or pay increments
  * Salary deduction (to compensate the aggrieved person)
  * Suspension or immediate termination of employment
* **Accountability for Malicious Complaints:** While complaints made in good faith that cannot be proven will **never** be penalized, knowingly filing a false or malicious complaint is a serious violation and will attract disciplinary action.

## 4. Preventing Harassment in My Own Behaviour

* **The "Family Test":** If I wouldn’t say, share, or do something with a close family member standing right next to me, I shouldn't do it at work.
* **Respect Personal Space:** Avoid unnecessary physical contact unless you are certain it is welcome.
* **Read the Room:** Stop immediately if someone looks uncomfortable, pulls back, goes quiet, or walks away.
* **Drop the "Just a Joke" Excuse:** Humour at the expense of someone’s gender, body, or dignity has no place at work.
